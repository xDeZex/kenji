# What is the correct wording and attribution of Toyota Kata's "Five Questions Card" (Coaching Kata)?

**Finding**: There is a real, canonical "Five Questions Card" from Mike Rother's Toyota Kata work, and it does originate specifically with Rother (not TWI, not a generic Toyota internal practice, not a different author) — but issue #6's five-item list is a paraphrase, not the verbatim card, and it drops one of the five prompts outright. The corroborated card text is:

1. What is the target condition?
2. What is the actual condition now?
3. What obstacles do you think are preventing you from reaching the target condition? Which one are you addressing now?
4. What is your next step? (Next experiment / next PDCA cycle) What do you expect?
5. How quickly can we go and see what we have learned from taking that step?

Note the real structure is 5 numbered prompts, but #3 is actually two questions ("what obstacles" + "which one are you addressing now") bundled under one number, and #4 is likewise two questions ("what is your next step" + "what do you expect") bundled under one number. Several secondary sources split these differently when summarizing, which is almost certainly why informally-circulated versions (including issue #6's) don't line up cleanly at 1:1 with the "five questions."

Comparing issue #6's list to the real card:
| Issue #6 says | Real card says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "What is the target condition?" | "What is the target condition?" | Verbatim match |
| "What is the current condition?" | "What is the actual condition now?" | Close paraphrase |
| "What obstacles do you see?" | "What obstacles do you think are preventing you from reaching the target condition?" | Compressed paraphrase, meaning preserved |
| "Which one are you addressing?" | "...Which one are you addressing now?" (second half of real Q3) | Close paraphrase, correctly placed in sequence |
| "What do you expect to happen?" | "What is your next step? ... What do you expect?" (real Q4) | Keeps only half of Q4, drops "what is your next step" |
| *(missing)* | "How quickly can we go and see what we have learned from taking that step?" (real Q5) | **Dropped entirely** — issue #6's list has no equivalent |

So issue #6's list is a reasonable-effort paraphrase of the real card's first four prompts, but it silently drops "What is your next step?" and drops the entire fifth question (the go-and-see-what-was-learned follow-through). That's not a minor wording variance — the real card's purpose is to walk a full PDCA loop (target → actual → obstacle/which-one → next-step-and-expectation → go-see-what-was-learned), and a version missing the "next step" and "go see" prompts only covers diagnosis, not the loop's close.

**Attribution**: Confirmed as originating with Mike Rother's Toyota Kata work specifically — *Toyota Kata: Managing People for Improvement, Adaptiveness, and Superior Results* (Rother, 2009) and the companion *Toyota Kata Practice Guide* (Rother, 2017), both McGraw-Hill. The card is Rother's own copyrighted material, reproduced (with permission) by third-party lean organizations. One attribution found in the wild (Ottawa County's Creativity Playbook) credits it jointly to "Mike Rother and Rick Fleming," Rother's frequent collaborator on Coaching Kata materials — worth knowing if a stricter citation is ever needed, but it doesn't change that the source is Rother's Toyota Kata body of work, not TWI or an unrelated author. No evidence found that this specific card predates or derives from TWI (Training Within Industry); TWI's Job Instruction/Job Relations programs are a separate, older lineage that Rother's work draws on conceptually but the Five Questions Card itself is not a TWI artifact.

**Source**: Cross-corroborated across independent secondary sources that reproduce the card's wording consistently: AME (Association for Manufacturing Excellence), "Kata Resources" (ame.org/kata-resources); Ottawa County Creativity Playbook, "Coaching Cycle 5 Questions" (playbook.miottawa.org, attributing the card to "Mike Rother and Rick Fleming"); and consistent synthesis across Lean Enterprise Institute (lean.org, "The Five Coaching Kata Questions"), Gemba Academy (gembaacademy.com, "The Five Questions Card" / "Coaching Kata Essentials" series), and Rother's own materials referenced from his University of Michigan faculty page (websites.umich.edu/~mrother) and katatogrow.com. Primary sources (Rother's *Toyota Kata*, 2009, ch. 8, and the *Toyota Kata Practice Guide*, 2017) were not directly fetchable in full text during this research — the wording above rests on convergent secondary quotation, not a fetched excerpt of the book itself. If verbatim book-page citation is ever required, that still needs checking against a physical/library copy.

**Added**: 2026-08-07

Caveat: treat the exact punctuation/line-break rendering of the card as approximate — different reproductions format questions 3 and 4 as one line or two, and some versions render "actual condition now" as "current condition." The substance above (5 prompts, that exact sequence, that exact content, dropping neither the next-step nor the go-and-see question) is what's load-bearing, not character-for-character wording.
